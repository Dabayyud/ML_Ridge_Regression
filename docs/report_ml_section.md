Machine learning signal
We used ridge regression to forecast the following month's difference between the log total returns of PETS and the iShares FTSE 250 ETF. Both series include reinvested dividends, and the ETF's charges slightly lower the hurdle for PETS; the price index serves only the descriptive comparison above. Ridge penalises large coefficients, which limits overfitting when the sample is small and predictors are correlated. The nine inputs were the latest month's excess return, 12-month momentum, 63-day share-price volatility, the FTSE 250's three-month return, UK retail sales growth and CPI (each lagged one month to its release), the three-month GBP/USD change, the annual change in UK Google searches for "puppy", and a CMA vets-inquiry announcement marker. The first model was trained on March 2015 to February 2020 and refitted monthly on an expanding window, with the penalty chosen by time-series cross-validation inside each training window (FI4008 project group, 2026).

Across 79 forecasts for April 2020 to October 2026, out-of-sample R² was −2.2% relative to the historical-mean forecast (90% bootstrap interval −5.5% to −0.1%). Neither the Diebold–Mariano (p = 0.94) nor the Clark–West test (p = 0.92) found an improvement. Ridge predicted the direction correctly in 35 of 79 months (44%), versus 46% for the historical mean and 57% for an always-underperforming rule. Unpenalised least squares scored −60.4%. Ridge selected the strongest tested penalty in 39 of 79 windows, so its forecasts had a standard deviation of about 0.7%, against 9.2% for realised excess returns: the penalty stopped the model fitting noise, but found no information to replace it. All five robustness checks were also negative, from −1.9% to −27.6% (FI4008 project group, 2026).

Interpreting the model
Holding PETS only when the forecast was positive returned −0.60 in cumulative log excess return, against −0.46 for holding PETS throughout, after a cost of 0.25% per switch (half of a 0.5% round trip). The model therefore receives zero weight under the pre-set rule, which required a positive and statistically significant R² to earn 25% weight. Power is limited: a planted signal with a true correlation of 0.3 was detected in only about a third of runs, so the result shows an absence of usable evidence rather than proof that no signal exists. Revised ONS series may differ from what investors knew at the time, and seven CMA events provide little basis for learning a repeatable response. The test covers one-month relative returns on one share, so it offers limited evidence about a 12-month absolute return. The investment decision rests on the operating evidence and valuation.
